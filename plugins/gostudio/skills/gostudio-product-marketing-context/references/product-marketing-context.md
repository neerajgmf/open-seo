### What "Your" means — read this before writing any copy

**"Your" signals ownership and authorship — not that your face is in the output.**

This is the single most important framing decision in this document. "Your" scales across every
customer because it describes *who the work belongs to*, not *who is in the picture*:

- A seller → **your** product
- A marketer → **your** brand's content
- A creator → **your** face

All three are "Your AI Studio" without the word meaning anything different. The through-line is
*"You command. AI executes."* — you are the director, the models are the crew.

**Do not** write "Your" as a promise that the output contains the user's likeness. That reading is
narrower than the product and excludes most of the customer base.

## Values & Philosophy — Why GoStudio Wins

*Added 2026-08-19. This section is the strategic foundation; everything below it is execution.*

**The premise.** Every AI platform now reaches the same underlying models. Feature parity is the
baseline, not an advantage, and a small team cannot win a capability race against funded
competitors. So GoStudio does not compete on capability alone.

**The analogy that drives this.** Political parties win individual elections on manifestos —
programmes, promises, specific giveaways. What makes a party *survive across decades* is
**ideology**. Supporters who share the ideology stay through a weak manifesto. Features are a
manifesto. Values are an ideology. GoStudio competes on the second.

---

### Pillar 1 — Values are the moat

We say plainly what we are: **a team of eight in Bengaluru, no venture capital, no large marketing
budget.** That is not an apology — it is the reason for a set of promises a funded competitor
cannot credibly make.

- **Real support from the people who build it.** Not a ticket queue.
- **No investor pressure to pivot, extract, or exit.** What we sell you next year is what we sell
  you today.
- **Low overheads mean thin margins by choice, and value passed on.** We cannot out-discount
  VC-funded competitors, and we do not claim to. We claim that what you pay gets you more.
- **We refuse work that harms people.** No NSFW, no nudes, no deepfakes, no non-consensual editing.
  This has cost us paying customers and we kept the policy.

**Credibility rule — values need receipts.** "We care more" is unfalsifiable and every small team
says it. Only make a values claim that points at something checkable. The strongest proof we have
is the NSFW refusal, precisely *because it has a price* — a commitment that costs nothing convinces
no one. Others: a public changelog with attribution, a reachable founder, a stated support
response, credit for community contributions.

**Handling the vendor-risk objection.** For creators, "small team" reads as trustworthy. For an
agency or brand evaluating a vendor, it can read as risk. The answer is track record and control,
not scale: started from zero and got here, based in a deep talent market, able to expand when
needed, and not dependent on investor timelines. Do not amplify smallness without pairing it with
durability.

---

### Pillar 2 — Use-case alignment and radical simplicity

**The analogy.** Give someone stairs and they want a lift. Give a lift and they want a faster one,
then an escalator. Expectation ratchets permanently upward. Model access *is* the stairs now —
table stakes, not a product.

The winner is whoever aligns the product to **real jobs** and makes them trivially simple:

- One-click solutions for the jobs people actually have.
- An extensive template library that pre-makes the creative decisions.
- A landing page per use case, so the product is found at the moment of need.
- Ranked in Google *and* cited by answer engines.

This is the strategy that already works for GoStudio and it is carried forward deliberately.

**The analogy.** The digital camera was one technology available to everyone. The value was never
in the camera — it was created by the people who used it. Models are today's digital camera.

So the platform's job is to let creators create the value, and to pay them for it:

- Creators build high-quality visuals and templates on GoStudio.
- **When others use a creator's template, 10% of the credits spent goes to that creator —
  paid in credits** (founder-confirmed 2026-08-20, modelled against `docs/pricing.json`).
- Full platform access and a direct line to the team.
- Content creation and the affiliate programme are being revamped as a **major pillar**, not a
  side channel.

**The promise underneath:** everyone grows with AI, including the people whose work makes the
platform worth using.

### Pillar 4 — No AI slop

We will not add noise to an already-polluted internet.

**The testable rule:** *a page ships only if it answers a real searched question and has a working
one-click tool behind it.* A page with a genuine tool behind it is not slop, however many exist.
A page that exists to catch a keyword is, however good the copy.

This rule is what reconciles Pillar 2 (many use-case pages) with this pillar. Without it, the
growth strategy quietly becomes the thing this pillar refuses to be.

## Photos vs Images — Core Product Distinction

GoStudio draws a deliberate line between **Photos** and **Images**. This distinction drives page
architecture, keyword targeting, and copy framing across the site. Never blur this line in copy.

**Photos** — photorealistic outputs of real-world subjects (people, pets, products).
- Includes both identity-preserving generation (trained model, always looks like you) and
  generic photorealistic generation (no training required).
- Pages: AI Photo Generator (two modes — trained / untrained), AI Photo Editor, headshot packs,
  product photography tools.
- User intent: "I want a realistic photo."

**Images** — creative, artistic, non-photorealistic outputs (digital art, illustrations,
stickers, avatars, concept art, wallpapers).
- No identity training required. Generic generative model.
- Pages: AI Image Generator.
- User intent: "I want to create something artistic or creative."

**Why this matters for copy:**
- "AI Image Generator" page targets art/creative intent — do NOT use photorealistic or
  identity-preservation language there.
- "AI Photo Generator" page targets realism intent — do NOT use art/creative language there.

**The dividing line is output type — realistic vs artistic — not identity training.**
Updated 2026-08-19. This distinction previously rested on identity preservation, with the upgrade
path "generic realistic photo → trained model → always looks like you" presented as the reason the
photo side mattered. Identity training is now a feature rather than the headline, so the line is
drawn where it actually sits for the user: **what kind of output do you want?** A photorealistic
result and an illustration are different jobs with different search intent, regardless of whether
any model was trained.

Identity training is available on the photo side and is worth mentioning there as a *capability* —
it is no longer the reason the photos/images split exists.

## Prompts vs Templates vs Tools — Surface Nomenclature

Three product surfaces, three different things. They are routinely confused because all three
deliver "a good result without figuring it out yourself." Use these definitions exactly; do not
treat the words as interchangeable in copy, page architecture, or internal docs.

**Prompts** — pre-written prompt text, organised by the model it is written for: Seedream,
Seedance, GPT Image, Nano Banana, and others as they are added. Lives on its **own page**. A prompt
is text only: the user copies it and runs it. Model names appear here because that is the surface's
whole point — see the reconciliation note below.
- User intent: *"Give me a prompt that works for the model I'm using."*
- **This page exists for SEO/AEO discovery, not as a destination.** Its job is to capture people
  searching for prompts by model name and route them onward to **templates and tools** — the
  surfaces where GoStudio has already done the curation. Every prompt page should carry that
  onward path. It is top-of-funnel, and it is not where the value is delivered.
- **Open pages — no gate, no account, no credits** (founder, 2026-08-20). Gating would defeat the
  only reason the surface exists. Prompts must be crawlable, copyable, and readable by both search
  engines and answer engines without a login.
- **Published in two places: GoStudio.ai and GitHub.** The prompt library runs as a public GitHub
  repository as well as on-site, both for traffic. GitHub is a real acquisition surface here — it
  ranks, it is where people already look for prompt collections, and it earns links and citations a
  marketing page does not. Treat the two as one library with two front doors: the GitHub copy must
  carry the same onward path back to templates and tools, or it captures the audience and hands it
  to nobody. This is currently the **only** channel in the acquisition mix that is not a page on
  our own domain.
- **Data file: `docs/prompts.json` and `docs/templates.json` are the same file shape** (founder,
  2026-08-20) — a template record is a prompt record with more fields filled in (references,
  placeholders). **The data model confirms the product definition:** a template is not a different
  kind of object from a prompt, it is a *completed* one. Keep the schemas in step; a field added for
  templates should be an optional field on the shared shape, not a fork.

**Templates** — a prompt **plus reference images**, with **placeholders** for the settings a user
is expected to change. A template is a prompt that has been finished: the references pin the look,
the placeholders mark the handful of decisions left to the user. Built against **SOTA models** —
templates are where the best available model is used, because the reference-and-prompt combination
is what makes the output worth pinning.
- User intent: *"Give me this exact look, with my subject in it."*
- Image templates and video templates are both first-class. Owner file: `docs/templates.json`.

**One-Click AI Tools** — **the only term for this surface.** "One-click apps" was a synonym in
internal use and is **retired**: do not use it in copy, page architecture, data files, or internal
docs. "One-Click AI Tools" wins because it already names Capability 2 and matches
`docs/ai-tools.json`. Built around a **use case**, not around a model. A tool wraps whatever model does that job best in
practice, and **the model behind a tool may not be the SOTA model** — it is chosen for cost or for
better output on that specific job.
- User intent: *"Do this job for me."*
- Owner file: `docs/ai-tools.json`.

**The dividing line: templates are pinned to SOTA models with reference images; tools are pinned to
a use case and may run a different model entirely.** The same nominal job can therefore produce
different results on the two surfaces. Copy must never imply a tool and a template give the same
output, and must never name a specific model on a tool page — the tool's model is an implementation
choice that changes.

**Routing.** A use-case landing page sends the user to **either** a template (image or video) **or**
a tool, depending on which surface actually serves that job. This is a real fork in the funnel:
the Acquisition Funnel Architecture section describes the tool path in full, and the template path
is the same shape with `templates.json` behind it instead of `ai-tools.json`.

> **The prompts page names models. That does not reinstate model hunting — do not "fix" it that
> way.** "No model hunting" is a promise about the **curated surfaces**: on templates and tools,
> GoStudio picks the model, so the user **never has to** choose. The prompts page is a **discovery
> and SEO/AEO surface** that captures search intent people already have — "seedream prompts", "nano
> banana prompt" — where the model name *is* the keyword. Someone searching for a Seedream prompt
> has already picked their model; meeting them there and routing them to the surfaces where they
> never have to is the funnel, not a contradiction of it.
>
> **Say "never has to choose", not "cannot choose."** Direct model access exists for subscribers —
> see the note below. The promise has always been the removal of a *requirement*, not the removal
> of an *option*. Copy that says choosing is impossible is inaccurate and will be contradicted by
> the product.

**Curated by default; direct model access for subscribers who want it.**
Alongside the curated surfaces, the platform gives **subscribers direct access to the underlying
models**, so power users who want hands-on control can work with them directly.

**This is a marketable claim (decided 2026-08-20), and the axis it is marketed on is
consolidation — not model access.** The pitch:

> **One platform, every handpicked model. No juggling ten different platform subscriptions.**

**The model set is deliberately narrow — say "handpicked", never "all".** GoStudio carries **only
handpicked models, each chosen as either the most cost-effective or the best available for its
job.** That two-criteria rule is the same principle the product runs on everywhere: templates are
pinned to the best model, tools may run a cheaper one that wins on that specific job (see "Prompts
vs Templates vs Tools"). Copy must never imply *every model on the market* — a short, tested,
deliberately-chosen roster is the claim, and it is a stronger one.

**Why that is not a return to model aggregation.** The claim GoStudio makes is *"stop paying three
subscriptions"* — which the document already owns at the H1, the boilerplate, and the pricing
wedge ("no juggling subscriptions", "no subscriptions to juggle", "not another subscription to
manage"). The claim it does **not** make is *"choose from 50 models"* — that is Group 3 and Group 6,
and the fight the competitive frame was deliberately rewritten to stop picking. Consolidation and
aggregation point in opposite directions: one removes decisions and bills, the other adds options.
Stay on the consolidation axis and this strengthens the existing position instead of undercutting
it.

Rules for writing it:

- **Prefer "juggling platform subscriptions" over naming providers.** The generic phrasing is the
  canonical one for a reason beyond tone: it says *stop paying for ten scattered tools* without
  telling anyone to cancel a specific service, which is the accurate claim. Name providers only
  when the copy genuinely needs them, and then apply the next rule.
- **If copy does name ChatGPT, Gemini, or Google, bound the substitution to visual work. This is
  the highest-risk sentence in the claim.** Those subscriptions also buy chat, writing, code,
  reasoning, and file analysis — GoStudio replaces none of that. The honest form is **"you don't

- **"Every handpicked model" is not "50 models to choose from," and not "every model."** The access

  reachable directly, not dumped on the user as a decision. Pair the two halves the way line 523
  already does: *not 50 models you have to choose from, and not another subscription to manage.*
- **Two different "no subscription" claims — do not merge them.** (a) *No subscription required to
  use GoStudio* — you can buy one-time credit packs; this is the pricing wedge, see Pricing
  Strategy. (b) *No subscriptions to the ten other platforms you would otherwise juggle* — this new
  claim, which is about **external** spend only. Both are true and they reinforce each other, but a
  copywriter who blends them produces "GoStudio is free", which is false and will be contradicted
  at checkout.
- **Qualitative until the page is built; the numbers get sourced then.** The claim is publishable as
  written. Any *figure* attached to it — "$20/mo × 3", "save $X a year", or a literal count of

  and unverified figures are not publishable. **Founder's instruction (2026-08-20): source the

- **It does not soften "no model hunting."** Both are true because they address different users:

- **It is not an invitation to the anti-ICP.** This serves a **subscriber with a job who wants

Individuals and small businesses doing everyday visual work — **primarily photo editing** —

Two groups, refused for different reasons.

**People seeking deepfakes, sexual imagery of women, or any non-consensual editing.**

#### 2. Fun-only users — not served, not refused

**People creating random images for amusement, with no job to do.**

Not a values problem — simply not the customer. They consume free credits and support capacity,

## Pricing Strategy & Positioning

The rationale behind the price points above. This is the strategic intent — use it when
writing pricing-page, homepage, and acquisition copy so messaging stays coherent with the
business model.

**The market gap we target — the casual, low-commitment user.** Competitors (Freepik,
Higgsfield, and most AI-model aggregators) have converged on subscription-only pricing that
starts high — ~$20+/month in the US, with similar floors elsewhere. That prices out a large,
underserved segment: users who want **5–10 images, not a monthly commitment.** GoStudio's
wedge is to serve exactly that user — low entry price, one-time packs, no subscription
required to access the models. This is coherent with the "no subscription required"
positioning on the homepage (§5, §8).

**One-time packs are the default for casual users; subscription is earned, not pushed.**
- One-time credit packs are the front door for the 5–10-image user — and they carry the
  healthiest margins (low-end packs run ~79–90% in `pricing.json`).
- The low-price subscription (entry price in `pricing.json`) is positioned as the obvious
  choice for a user who has *already returned* — a reward for proven repeat use, not a default
  funneled to first-time visitors.

**Framing rule — honest value, never "they'll forget to cancel."** A low price wins because
the commitment sits below the user's decision-friction threshold *and* because it is genuinely
worth keeping. Copy, pricing design, and lifecycle comms must make the value obvious — never
rely on, hint at, or internally optimize for breakage (users forgetting an active
subscription). Breakage revenue is fragile, generates churn/chargebacks/bad reviews, and
contradicts GoStudio's values-driven brand. The durable version of the strategy: price so low
that keeping it is a no-brainer because it delivers, not because cancelling is annoying.

**Local / PPP pricing.** Price floors are localized for payment markets where a US-level price
is a hard no (e.g. India). This expands the funnel; see "Geographic & Channel Strategy."

**Discounts.** Price low *structurally*; do not run a permanent "70% off"-style slash.
Always-on discounts anchor perceived value low, train users to wait, and read as fake urgency.
A genuine, time-bound intro offer is fine; permanent discount theatre is not.

**Economic constraint — the low-ticket business cannot afford paid acquisition.** After
discounts and payment-processing fees (~$0.30 + ~3% per transaction), low-ticket ARPU is thin.
The model only works on near-zero CAC — i.e. organic search + AEO. This is consistent with the
SEO/AEO-first growth strategy; it also means paid-ads spend is not viable for the casual tier.

**Casual tier is a front door, not the whole house.** The 5–10-image user is low-commitment by
nature — cheap to win, cheap to lose. (**Not** low-*intent*: they intend to buy, they just do not
intend to subscribe. See the Anti-ICP section, which turns on exactly this distinction.) The strategy depends on an **upgrade hook**: converting
a slice of casual users into "this is my studio" users who discover identity training and the
full studio breadth. Casual acquisition without an upgrade path is a leaky bucket.

**B2B is a future bet, not a pivot.** Long-term intent is to interview B2B customers and
explore where GoStudio expands. Consumer low-ticket (self-serve + AEO) and B2B (sales-led) are
different motions — B2B is a deliberate, interview-validated future bet, while consumer
low-ticket remains the near-term cash-flow, brand, and AEO engine. Do not run both as co-equal
priorities prematurely.

## "Built With You" — Co-Creation Pillar

Customers should feel they belong here and that GoStudio is *their* studio, not just a tool they pay for. The brand truth is real — Kanika launched on Reddit asking for feedback, turned away NSFW money, and ships based on customer asks. The job is to make this visible in product, copy, and operations.

**Brand-level statement:** "Your AI Studio. Built Together."
**Operational tagline (every feature release):** "You asked. We built it."
**Founder-ethos statement (About page):** "Built solo from a tier-3 city. Now built with the people who use it."

**Meaning of "Your AI Studio" in this pillar:** The studio belongs to users in two ways: they use it to create their own photos, videos, and daily AI outputs; and they help shape what gets built next. This must feel like a product relationship, not a generic community claim.

**Do NOT** use generic community language ("we love our community," "we listen to our customers"). Every SaaS says it; it signals nothing. The mechanisms below *show* the belief without claiming it.

### Mechanisms (in order of priority and cheapness)

1. **Changelog with attribution.** Every shipped feature credits the customer(s) who asked for it: *"Built because @username asked for this."* Lives on a public changelog page, appears in in-product release notes, and is reposted to social. Cheapest, highest-signal mechanic — ship this first.

2. **Kanika building in public.** Founder visibility is the single biggest lever for psychological ownership in indie products. Weekly post (X / LinkedIn / in-product banner): "This week we shipped X. [User] asked. We tested. Now live." Without the founder visible, the rest is theater.

3. **About / Founder Story page.** Kanika's actual arc — tier-3 origin → system failed her → self-taught with AI + YouTube → moved to Bangalore → 8-person team building together. Manifesto-style, not corporate. Anchors the *why* the product exists for *you*, not for shareholders. Use the Reddit-selfie energy (real home, real grit) — not a polished founder portrait.

4. **Public roadmap with voting.** Canny / Featurebase / Linear public boards. Customers submit asks, vote on others' asks, see what's coming. They co-decide the path, not just consume it.

5. **Onboarding language that reinforces ownership.** "Welcome to your studio" beats "Welcome to GoStudio." "Made for the moments you told us about" beats "Powered by AI." Small word choices repeat the frame every session.

6. **User-named templates.** When a template or workflow is built from a customer ask, credit that customer or profession in the template name and description: *"This portrait template was requested by [user] for [profession]."* Permanent artifact of community contribution. Costs nothing.

7. **Community channel.** Discord / Telegram / in-product. Highest investment — only ship if moderation can keep pace. Bad community is worse than no community. Lower priority than the others.

### Banned phrases (co-creation context)

- "We love our community."
- "We listen to our users."
- "Empower" / "Democratize" (already banned globally; reinforced here)
- Any co-creation claim without a visible mechanism backing it. If the mechanism isn't live, don't make the claim.

## Common Objections

**"Why not just hire a photographer?"**
→ A GoStudio plan (current prices in `pricing.json`) vs $200-500 per session. 15 minutes vs 2 hours. Train once, generate forever. No booking, no scheduling, no location constraints.

**"Will it look like me?"**
→ Trained on your face, body, and style — preserved across every image and video model in our curated stack. You always look like you, every output, every time. Not a filter, not an approximation — your actual identity, preserved across every model we picked for the job.

**"Will my product look consistent across my catalog?"**
→ Train your product once. Every photo generated from that model maintains the same consistent look — same angles, same quality, same brand aesthetic.

**"Why not use ChatGPT or Gemini?"**
→ They give you ingredients. We give you the finished meal. Model selection, prompt engineering, workflow optimization — all done. You get one-click professional results.
→ **Do not** answer this with an identity-quality claim. Base models handle likeness well now; the honest and stronger answer is workflow, templates, and consistency of result.

**"Why is a team of eight a safe bet?"** *(agency / brand prospects)*
→ Bootstrapped and profitable-minded, with no investor pressure to pivot or exit — what we sell you next year is what we sell you today. Started from zero and built it. Based in Bengaluru with access to deep talent, and we expand when the work requires it.

**"Why should I trust you over a bigger platform?"**
→ We turn away work that harms people — no NSFW, no deepfakes, no non-consensual editing — and that has cost us paying customers. We publish what we ship and who asked for it. Support comes from the people who built the product.

**"Why not use PhotoRoom or Canva?"**
→ They edit photos. We generate brand-consistent identity. The output isn't an edited photo — it's a professionally generated image that looks like you or your product, every time.

> **Scope of "keep the stack backstage" — the prompts page is the exception, by design.**

> - **The identity-preservation stack (Photalabs) — never named in consumer copy at all.**
> - **Tool pages — never name the model behind a tool.** That model is an implementation choice
>   picked for cost or output and it changes; naming it turns an internal decision into a promise.

> It does **not** cover two surfaces where model names are the point:
> - **The prompts page**, which names public image and video models — Seedream, Seedance, GPT

>   and the model name is the search term.
> - **Direct model access for subscribers**, where a power user selects a model by name. You cannot
>   offer a choice without naming the options.
>
> Both are covered in "Prompts vs Templates vs Tools". Naming a model on a discovery page, or to a
> subscriber who has chosen to work hands-on, is not asking a customer to choose one in order to
> get their job done — which is what this rule exists to prevent.
